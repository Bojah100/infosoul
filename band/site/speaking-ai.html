The AI age, for the layman.

What is happening, what it means, how to use it, and what is worth arguing about

One talk, for people who are not engineers. A plain-language orientation to the age we are already in — how the technology works, who owns it, what it is doing to work, and the questions that come with it. From somebody who builds these systems for a living and is not selling you one from the stage.

What the room gets

  • A brief history of AI — where it actually came from, the winters and the breakthroughs, and why it arrived in public all at once when it had been coming for seventy years.
  • The tech, broken down — in plain speech, no maths, and what the scores actually mean.
  • Using it — real cases, demonstrated live, including running a free model on your own laptop with no company in the middle.
  • What it gets wrong — sycophancy, and invention: a machine that produces a convincing record is not a nuisance when the record is somebody’s life.
  • Who owns it, and what it means for work — the money, the data centres, what is displacing and what is newly possible without capital.

Not a sales pitch, not a doom lecture, not a demo. The room leaves able to use one, and able to tell when it is flattering them.
