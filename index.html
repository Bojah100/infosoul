The Analogy

You already trust this model. It used to just be called IT.

Before managed IT providers existed, small organizations either couldn't afford an in-house tech department or muddled through alone. MSPs showed up with a simple pitch: we'll manage your servers, backups, security, and helpdesk — you don't need to understand any of it, you just need to trust us and pay a predictable fee. That industry is now worth well over $150 billion, built almost entirely on trust rather than technical evaluation.

Think about the last time your organization set up a new office suite, installed the apps everyone actually needed, configured the network, or loaded years of records into new accounting software. Chances are you didn't do that alone — you brought in a specialist, they handled it, and you got back to your actual work. AI workflows are no different. You could spend weeks figuring out which tools fit your organization and stitching them together yourself, or you could bring in a specialist to get you up and running.

That's the model we're bringing to AI. Call it AI IT.

Real Output — Open-Source, Self-Hosted, Generated On Real Hardware

This isn't a mockup. The face was generated by Segmind Tiny-SD (Apache 2.0), the voice by Piper (MIT), and the mouth movement by Wav2Lip — all open-source, self-hosted, no cloud API involved. The face is entirely synthetic and fictional; no real person's likeness was used. Hover over it — every frame of lip motion was generated from the audio, not a canned animation or a volume pulse.

Generated using open-source components we'd deploy the same way for you: Piper for voice (MIT), Wav2Lip for lip-sync (CC BY-NC 4.0 — non-commercial; a real deployment would use a commercially-licensed lip-sync engine instead). We couldn't run any of this inside our own demo sandbox — it has no GPU and its network is deliberately locked down — so we ran it on ordinary consumer hardware (a 4GB GPU) instead and pulled the result in. That's the honest version of what a real deployment looks like: matching the model to the hardware you actually have, which is exactly what our Hardware Fit tool above helps you plan for.

Before we build one: we get written consent from anyone whose voice or likeness is used, and every generated video is clearly labeled as AI-generated. That's non-negotiable — trust is the whole point.

The Talk

The AI Age, For the Layman.

Before an organization buys anything, somebody has to explain the thing. This is that explanation, given in your room, to your people — and it is the way most clients meet us.

Formats

  • 50 min — a class slot or a lunchtime session. 35 and 15 for questions.
  • 75 min — the evening talk. 50 and 25. Best for a general audience.
  • 90 min — hands-on. The room uses one, on their own question, and watches it get something wrong.
  • Half day — run your own: install an open-source model on your own machine. No subscription, no company reading your work.
  • Series — three or four sessions for a library, congregation, local or programme.

Who it is for

  • Libraries — staff training and public programming. Also archives and records people.
  • Unions — the live bargaining issue in every local, and few people speaking to members without selling something.
  • Campuses — general audience, first-year seminars, journalism, library science, ethics.
  • Churches — the moral and spiritual questions, which almost nobody is addressing.
  • Community — seniors, youth programmes, small business, job seekers.
